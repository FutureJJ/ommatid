In 2026 the complete wiring of a male fruit fly's nervous system was published: 165,122 neurons and every synapse between them, traced from one animal. Ommatid runs that wiring and gives it a body it never had.

What this is

The fly sees through the robot's camera, sampled onto its own retina of 721 columns per eye. The optic lobe is a connectome-constrained model of the fly visual system (Lappalainen et al. 2024). Everything downstream, the central brain and the ventral nerve cord, is the male connectome run as leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with the parameters of Shiu et al. 2024, with nothing trained and nothing tuned to behaviour. The robot moves when the descending neurons a fly walks with fire: DNa02 to steer, DNa01 to walk forward, MDN to back up, DNp09 to stop.

This is a body-transfer experiment. Phase 1 measures what survives the transfer untouched and where the mismatch lies. Phase 2 hands the legs to the nerve cord, with feedback from the robot's own joints. Phase 3 asks what an adapting nervous system needs to live in a new body at all. Three reflexes were pre-registered for phase 1; the results are public, including the failures.
