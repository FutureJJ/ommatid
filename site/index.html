What this is

In 2026 the complete wiring diagram of a male fruit fly's central nervous system was published: 165,122 neurons and every synapse between them, traced from electron microscope images of one animal. Ommatid runs that wiring as a simulation and gives it a body: a six-legged robot with a camera, standing in a room.

The fly sees through the robot's camera, sampled onto its own retina of 721 columns per eye. The optic lobe is a connectome-constrained model of the fly visual system (Lappalainen et al. 2024). Everything downstream, the central brain and the ventral nerve cord, is the male connectome run as leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with the parameters of Shiu et al. 2024, with nothing trained and nothing tuned to behaviour. The robot moves when the descending neurons a fly walks with fire: DNa02 to steer, DNa01 to walk forward, MDN to back up, DNp09 to stop.

The question is simple to state. If you give a wiring diagram eyes and legs, do the reflexes of the animal appear? Three are pre-registered: turning with the visual world (the optomotor response), stopping or backing away from an approaching object, and walking toward light. Each is tested against a control in which the same neurons are rewired at random. Failures will be reported as failures. The protocol, the honesty rules and the code are public.

The brain runs slower than the animal's: one 20 ms step of the fly's time takes about 130 ms of ours. The dilation is shown live above and reported with every result. The full design is in docs/experiment.md; what broke on the way and why is in docs/model.md.
