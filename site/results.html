Phase 1, protocol v1: none of the three hypotheses is established, and the one signal we saw turned out to be an artefact of our own rendering. This page keeps the numbers and says what they were.

Correction, 11 September 2026, after independent review. An earlier version of this page called the wiring-specificity contrast "met". That was an overstatement: the shuffled-wiring control also changed how camera input is mapped onto neurons (column positions of unlabeled cell types are inferred from the loaded graph), the analysis did not implement the pre-registered 200 ms onset criterion, it pooled controls that behave very differently, and 17 trials contained steps with no camera frame. The numbers below are unchanged; their status is now exploratory. A further check the same day showed the escape-neuron response itself was produced by our off-camera surround fill, which darkened the whole eye whenever a dark object entered the camera frame; with a fixed-grey surround the synthetic looming disc no longer drives the escape neurons at all (LC4 2 Hz, DNp04 0 Hz). See the run log, "The surround artefact". The fixes and the v2 protocol are in the run log.

What this does and does not show

It suggests that a dark object appearing in front of the fly reaches DNp04 and DNp02 through the pathway the anatomy predicts (LC4 → DNp04/DNp02), and that the response is weaker when the wiring is shuffled. It does not establish looming selectivity (the static disc evoked the same response), and it does not establish wiring specificity, because the shuffle also changed the input mapping. Nothing in the model was fitted to behaviour; the pathway was pre-registered, not searched for.

It does not show a fly that sees the world. The optic lobe is a task-trained connectome model (FlyVis), the central model has no baseline activity and uniform synaptic weights, and the robot's camera gives the fly 74° of a 300° field. Wide-field motion and phototaxis did not appear; the measured mismatches — field of view, a transient-only hand-off, no disinhibition from a silent baseline, a single steering neuron as readout — are the targets of the v2 protocol and of phases 2 and 3, in which the nerve cord drives the legs and adaptation is studied.

Failures are reported as failures; thresholds were not moved after the fact. The next runs are pre-registered in the run log.
